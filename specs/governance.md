# Governance

> **Status:** not started. Nothing is decided yet; this page records the problem and the
> questions to settle. Listed in the [index](README.md).

## Problem

Standard mods have an RFC process ([`mods.md`: Contribution and governance](mods.md#contribution-and-governance)), and the code is dual-licensed MIT
or Apache-2.0. The language itself has no defined way to change, no named decision makers, and no
plan for the community, security response or funding that a long-lived language needs.

## Touched on in

[`mods.md`: Contribution and governance](mods.md#contribution-and-governance), [`evolution.md`](evolution.md) (editions),
[`packages-and-supply-chain.md`](packages-and-supply-chain.md) (audit trail), [`security.md`](security.md) (security response).

## Questions to settle

- **Who decides language changes**: a single maintainer, a core team, or a foundation, and how that
  changes as the project grows.
- **An RFC process for the language**, not only for standard mods, tied to editions.
- **Agents in the process**: agent-written RFCs, implementations and reviews, and how their review
  records are marked.
- A **code of conduct** and moderation.
- **Trademark and the name** "Moddil", the tagline ("armour, not baggage") and the armadillo
  mascot and logo ([`adoption.md`](adoption.md)).
- **Funding** for the compiler, standard mods and infrastructure.
- **Security response**: who owns advisories and fixes ([`security.md`](security.md)).
